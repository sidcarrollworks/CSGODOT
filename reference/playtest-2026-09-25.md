## Across the issues

- **Dropped guns and ragdolls share a floor problem.** dust2's hull is a
  one-sided trimesh (`ConcavePolygonShape3D`, no backface collision); a
  body whose centre gets under a triangle is not pushed back. Every
  rigid-body check today lands on the range's box floor, which hid it:
  both 2 and 4 need checks on a one-sided trimesh floor and ramp.
- **E is one key for several things.** Picking up (3), defusing, CS2's
  planting by E, and taking the bomb from a bot (18) share `+use`; keep
  one precedence rule that `BombSystem` answers and `ItemDrops` asks.
- **Two import defaults.** Vertex compression (9) and mesh LODs (12) come
  from Godot's glTF import defaults, verified in Godot 4.7's source;
  `reference/godot/import.md` and `rendering.md` get the caveats in those
  pull requests.
- **The drawn body only.** Foot IK (5) and the twist bones (8) move the
  drawn body; nothing the simulation reads may depend on them, the

  whether `SkinnedHitboxes` (on `skeleton_updated`) sees a modifier.
- **Research still missing,** flagged per CLAUDE.md: dropped-weapon

## Confirmed on Sid's machine

Three causes checked by drawing dust2 on Sid's machine (RTX 4070 Ti,
2026-09-25), with no bots, a camera of its own at Sid's positions (the
HUD's feet position, 64 units up for the eye, Sid's yaw and pitch, the
game's field of view) and the HUD hidden, in a 1920x1080 window. The
scripts were scratch ones, not kept; what they did is here.

- **11, the flicker: Godot's occlusion culling, the sky's brushes most of
  it.** With the camera exactly still at Sid's spot (-44.0 1.5 -492.0, yaw
  260.1, pitch 9.1), 36 frames in a row were identical in every variant,
  occlusion on included: nothing flickers while the camera holds perfectly
  still. In play it never does, so 40 poses within 2 units and 1 degree
  of the spot were each drawn with every variant and compared with the
  same pose drawn with no culling at all (occlusion off and the map's
  visibility off), which is what should be seen:
  - occlusion off (the map's visibility on): all 40 poses match. The
    map's own visibility (`WorldVisibility`) hides nothing it should
    show here.
  - as is: all 40 poses miss something, up to 295 pixels: a part of the
    white building behind the tower, top right of the crosshair, where
    Sid saw the sign come and go.
  - the hull's `physics_sky` part left out of the occluders (266 of the
    occluders' 177,075 triangles): the building part comes back; up to
    85 pixels still differ, a sliver along a rooftop edge (a hull part
    standing past its drawn outline) and specks through the cracks of
    issue 9, which show whatever the culling left behind the wall.

  So leaving the sky out is most of the fix, as issue 11's plan says, and
  not all of it; the rest is the hull's outlines against the drawn ones.
  Whether to shrink the occluders or keep Godot's occlusion culling at all
  beside the map's visibility is the profiler's call (`no_occlusion`), as
  the plan says.

  Held poses under-state it. Sid sees the flicker while moving the view,
  so the camera was also swept smoothly, a new pose each frame (6 degrees
  of yaw, 3 of pitch), and each frame compared with the same frame drawn
  with no culling:
  - top of mid: every one of 150 moving frames misses something as is (up
    to 88 pixels in a quarter sample, every second pixel each way), 130
    with the sky left out (up to 22), about none with occlusion off.
  - lower mid, Sid's second spot (-594.0 1.9 -441.5, yaw 269.4, pitch
    -4.6, looking through the underpass towards mid doors): 91 of 150 as
    is (up to 140), 98 with the sky left out (up to 117): the sky is not
    the cause there. The largest miss was the inside of a window above the
    underpass; the rest were specks through issue 9's cracks. The run had
    no bots, so bodies or guns behind mid doors, if that is what Sid saw
    flicker, were not in it.

  Both spots agree: only Godot's occlusion culling loses anything, and
  the map's own visibility never does. Leaving the sky out fixes top of
  mid; at lower mid it takes shrinking the occluders or turning Godot's
  occlusion culling off, beside `WorldVisibility`, which
  `scripts/profile_render.gd`'s `no_occlusion` prices.
- **12, the tower stripes: mesh LODs.** B site from Sid's spot (1846.8
  221.7 -1907.2, yaw 261.2, pitch -0.6), drawn with the viewport's
  `mesh_lod_threshold` at its default 1.0 and at 0.0 (no LOD): the
  zigzag stripes on both kasbah towers are there with LODs and gone
  without them, and nothing else in the view changes.
- **9, the seam: vertex compression.** The two plaster pieces that meet
  at the seam (`n0_lr0_agg_merge_hr_dust_blend_plaster_white_01b2_014` and
  `_017`) are both stored compressed after import. Their nine vertices
  on the shared edge (Godot x -8, z -384, y 0 to 116), identical in the
  glTF, are 0.0226 units apart after import, all along the edge. That is
  the 16-bit step of `_017`, whose box is 40.4 of the export's units (the
  model's metres) wide: 40.4 / 65535 x 39.37 = 0.024 units. Drawn from
  Sid's spot (-44.0 1.5 -492.0, yaw 202.3, pitch 6.2), the edge shows as
  a dashed line of sky and sunlit wall through the plaster.

## 2. Dropped guns are points, not bodies: they sink into slopes, the magazine passes through the floor, and they spin about the wrong point

*Split, size L. Screenshot: image 1: a dropped SMG on the T spawn ramp, half of it under the asphalt.*

**What Sid saw.** Sid: a gun lying on the T spawn ramp has half its length through the ground, which also makes it harder to pick up. A thrown gun's magazine goes through the ground as if it had no physics at all, and the centre of mass looks wrong. Image 1 (41.webp) looks down at the asphalt road beside T spawn's red and white curb. A small black SMG lies on its side on the road with the crosshair next to it. From the outline it is a MAC-10 or MP9; I can't tell which. Only the stock, grip and receiver show. The front half is hidden under the road surface, well clear of the curb, so the gun is sunk into the sloped road and has not gone into the curb.

**Why.**

Several causes add up. Each is marked verified or inferred.

(A) VERIFIED (code): the simulation traces one point, not the gun. DroppedItem.tick (src/game/dropped_item.gd:115-135) casts one ray a tick from `position` (lines 120-122). Nothing else of the gun's extent is traced, so the magazine, stock and barrel pass through floors and walls in flight and on every bounce. On each bounce level() turns the gun upright (line 132, LEVEL_ON_BOUNCE 0.7), and at rest it puts `position` on the hit point (line 134). An upright gun whose origin sits on the floor has its magazine under the floor: the MP9's magazine, the vertices skinned to its `clip` bone, reaches 3.6 in below the model origin. It stays there until the view tips the gun over, 0.2 s later (dropped_item_view.gd SETTLE_SECONDS).

(B) VERIFIED (game files): the simulated point is the model's origin, which is not the centre of mass. I dumped CS2's physics with Source2Viewer-CLI `-b PHYS` on every weapons/models/*.vmdl_c, read-only (the dump is not in the repo; its numbers are given here, and plan step 1 makes it an extraction step). Every gun has one convex hull, bound to its `weapon_offset` bone (the Dual Berettas bind theirs to `weapon`). Mass is 3 to 6 (pistols 3, SMGs 3.5, rifles 4 to 4.5, snipers 5, machine guns 6). Damping is 0, except angular damping of 10 on the Glock and 8 on the flashbang, smoke, decoy, molotov and incendiary. Drag is 1 and m_bOverrideMassCenter is false. The centre of mass in m_MassProperties matches the volume centroid I recomputed from the triangles of the extracted *_physics.gltf hulls, and the volumes match m_flVolume.
In Godot model axes (x = the gun's left, y = up, z = muzzle, inches), the centre of mass sits this far from the model origin: MP9 (0, 2.19, 2.16), 3.1 in. AK-47 6.2. AWP 14.2. G3SG1 20.4. M4A4 (0, 10.36, 17.72), 20.5. SCAR-20 23.1.
DroppedItem turns `basis` about `position`, the model origin (lines 113-114). So every gun spins about the wrong point, and the long guns orbit empty space. That is the centre of mass Sid sees.

(C) VERIFIED (code and files): a drop jumps the drawn gun away from the hand. HeldPose.of (src/game/held_pose.gd) returns the hand's `wpn` bone, and the third-person body pins the gun's root `weapon` bone there (src/player/player_model.gd:190-212). DroppedItemView._place (src/game/dropped_item_view.gd:119-124), however, puts the model's origin on that point. The drawn gun therefore moves off by the `weapon` bone's rest offset at the moment it is dropped: MP9 10.0 in, P90 12.1, G3SG1 18.9, M4A4 22.1, SCAR-20 22.0. The light probe is also sampled at the model origin (lines 87 and 113), not where the gun is drawn.

(D) VERIFIED (code), with the slope INFERRED: the view makes up the resting pose. DroppedItemView.lying() (lines 193-205) turns the model about its origin onto its thinnest bind-pose side, then lifts it straight up until the box's lowest corner is at the contact height (lines 126-133). It never learns the ground normal, because DroppedItem does not keep it, and nothing traces the drawn shape.
On a slope the gun lies level, so its uphill end goes into the ground. Dust2's collision export has concrete floors of 11 to 15 degrees within 900 units of the T spawns (histogram from world_physics_physics.gltf). At 14 degrees the ground rises about 2.7 in over half a gun's length. A gun lying on its side is only 1 to 2 in thick, so its uphill half is buried, as in Image 1.
The drawn gun's middle also sits away from `position`, the point pickup measures from, by the bind pose's own offset: M4A4 21.4 in, G3SG1 22.7, SCAR-20 25.9, AWP 17.8, SG 553 11.8.

(E) VERIFIED (code), with the geometry INFERRED: pickup fails on slopes. ItemDrops._try_pickup (src/game/item_drops.gd:125-127) refuses an item more than 1 unit below the player's origin. On a slope the player's 32-unit box stands on its uphill edge, so its origin sits about 16 x tan(slope) above the ground under its middle: about 4 in at 14 degrees. Standing on the gun, or uphill of it, does not pick it up. Together with (D), that is why it is harder to pick up.

(F) VERIFIED (files), minor: the pose used for drawing is wrong for some guns. Every world model carries a 'dropped' clip, and the pistols also carry 'dropped_empty', whose slide is back. The view draws the bind pose instead. For most guns the dropped pose only moves the root bone, which does not matter once the gun is drawn by its physics bone. The Dual Berettas' bind pose, though, holds the two pistols about 22 in apart, and their dropped clip lays them side by side.

Not the cause: CS2's separate *_mag models, which have their own hulls, are not drawn on a dropped gun here. The magazine Sid saw is part of the gun's own mesh.

2. 2. Load the table at startup. Remote. Read physics.csv once at load, never in the tick, the way ItemRegistry reads vdata.csv. Build one ConvexPolygonShape3D per class from it. Where a class has no row, fall back to a box centred on the origin, as the stand-in is today.
3. 3. Simulate the gun's body at its centre of mass. Remote. 4. 4. Collide the hull instead of a point. Remote. While the gun moves, sweep its hull each tick on WORLD_LAYER only, as grenades do; CS2's behaviour at player clips is still to be measured. Resolve each contact with an impulse at the contact point, using the inertia, restitution and friction, so a gun that lands on its muzzle tumbles about its centre of mass. 4. INFERRED: what pushes a capsule's centre under the floor. (a) Jolt's continuous collision detection (continuous_cd=true, ragdoll.gd:254) only sweeps straight-line motion (physics.md:243 gives its thresholds as fractions of a body's inner radius), so a shin or foot whipping round can pass through. (c) An extra neck body (hitbox radius 3.5, 1.4 in long) that CS2's ragdoll does not have. (d) The joint friction is written straight into angular_velocity from _physics_process, outside the solver (ragdoll.gd:204-216).
5. VERIFIED by reading: why joints bend too far. Every cone joint is centred on the pose at the moment of death, not on a natural rest pose. _join/_cone take the limb's direction from the death pose (ragdoll.gd:305-341), and Godot fixes a joint's frames from the bodies' positions when the joint is made. So a hip that died mid-stride 30 degrees back can swing 50 more degrees back. The cones are also the same in every direction (ConeTwistJoint3D has one swing span, physics.md:290-293). The hip therefore bends as far back and sideways as forward, and the shoulder 70 degrees any way from a rifle-holding pose. The head (30) and neck (25) add up to 55 degrees of head tilt. The knee and elbow hinges are set correctly, from straight to 125 and 120 degrees (ragdoll.gd:344-359). Body parts never collide with each other, since the mask is the world only (ragdoll.gd:30-31), so limbs can pass through the torso and through each other (INFERRED to add to the tangled look).
6. VERIFIED: CS2's own ragdoll shapes are already extracted. tm_phoenix_varianta.vmdl:1976-2130 and ctm_sas.vmdl each hold a PhysicsShapeList: 12 capsules and 3 spheres on 15 bones, surface playerflesh. CS2's playerflesh surface is friction 0.8, elasticity 0.25, density 900 (reference/surfaces/surfaces.csv:149); the ragdoll uses friction 0.9 and bounce 0 (ragdoll.gd:259-262).

## 7. The tarp on xbox is far too dark

*Split, size M. Screenshot: image 5: the tarp on xbox, nearly black olive (pos 1384 -28 -357, yaw 201, pitch -48).*

**What Sid saw.** Image 5 (pos 1384.0 -28.0 -357.2, yaw 201.1, pitch -48.5, looking down at xbox in mid): the tarp on top is a flat, dark olive with no sun or shadow shapes on it. The wooden crates right beside it are sunlit and bright.

**Why.**

Two causes, both verified from the extracted data and the code; how much each shows on screen is inferred.

(1) The tarp reads its baked light from the wrong UV set. It is glTF mesh `n0_lr0_agg_merge_tarp_kit_01_base_0_fragment1`, material `tarp_kit_01_base` (models/generic/tarp_kit_01/tarp_kit_01_base.vmat), shader csgo_environment.vfx, the only csgo_environment material on dust2. Its TEXCOORD_1 is exactly (0,0) at every vertex. Its lightmap is TEXCOORD_2 (CUSTOM0): median 0.747 texels a unit at 8192, the same as the world's 0.75. S2V's csgo_environment.vert.slang always carries the model's own second set (vTEXCOORD1), which pushes the lightmap to the third set. But `LightmapMaterials.apply` (src/map/lightmap_materials.gd:94-132) only reads CUSTOM0 when F_FORCE_UV2 is set, and that flag is not on this material. For an all-collapsed set, `chart_density` returns 0, which passes the density filter, so every pixel samples the lightmap at UV (0,0). I read those texels: irradiance (0.84, 0.63, 0.54), and in direct_light_shadows.png the sun's channel is 255 (blocked). Since R4 the map is out of the live shadow map, so the tarp never gets the sun, only a constant bounce. A scan of all 1,700 lightmapped prop primitives found this is the only one with an all-zero UV2.

(2) The tint is applied as a plain multiply. S2V's glTF exporter (GltfModelExporter.Material.cs, GenerateGLTFMaterialFromRenderMaterial) writes the draw call's tint as a linear multiply: baseColorFactor [0.178, 0.198, 0.144], about sRGB (118, 124, 105). Godot imports that as albedo_color, and `LightmapMaterials.build` carries it over. CS2's csgo_environment, per S2V's reimplementation (csgo_environment.frag.slang ColorizeTint and GetMaterial; .vert.slang lines 153-160), recolours the texture to the tint's hue and keeps its luminance. The amount is g_flModelTintAmount*(1-min3(tint)), masked by g_tHeight1's green through ContrastBrightness(contrast 4, brightness 4). The tarp texture is near-white (mean sRGB 222, linear about 0.73). That gives about (0.32, 0.35, 0.28) linear, where the multiply gives (0.13, 0.145, 0.105): 2.5 times darker even before the missing sun.

Extraction: the world export is from 2026-09-21, before CS2's VCS 72 update, so the tarp's glTF material came through whole. Its g_tHeight1 mask was never extracted, because the glTF has no slot for it; that is not a VCS 72 loss.

**Already covered.** None. asset-pipeline.md's "dust2 lightmapped props" row states the F_FORCE_UV2 rule only. csgo_environment is listed in LightmapMaterials.PROP_SHADERS, and no page says how tints are applied. No research page covers csgo_environment or model tints; the reference is Source 2 Viewer's public reimplementation. Related: inferno uses csgo_environment on 335 materials plus 365 csgo_environment_blend, so this is groundwork for inferno.

**Depends on.** 12 (the LOD fix must cover CUSTOM0-lit surfaces, and the tarp becomes one). Related to 10 (tints and saturation).

**Plan.**

## 23. The distant hill is missing: the 3D skybox cut at the far plane

*Split, size S. Screenshots: two, sent after the first 22, from T spawn
towards mid (pos -1060.0 128.4 -1044.1, yaw 301.8, pitch 8.6): CS2's with
a hazy hill behind the city, ours with only sky there.*

**What Sid saw.** "The distant hill/mountain is invisible. They seem to be
out of the render range or something."

**Why.** Verified on Sid's machine, 2026-09-25, by drawing Sid's view as in
"Confirmed on Sid's machine":

- Neither culling hides it. With Godot's occlusion culling off the frame is
  the same to the pixel; with the map's visibility off, 4,843 pixels of
  nearby buildings change and the hill is still missing; leaving the sky's
  brushes out of the occluders changes nothing. With the camera's far plane
  at 100,000 units rather than 16,384 (`player_view.gd`, `camera.far`), the
  hill is there.
- What lies past the far plane from there is the 3D skybox
  (`world_vwrld_c`, every mesh on the far shader, `FarMaterials`). Of the
  28 meshes ahead of the view that reach past 12,000 units, the terrain
  pieces `node000_lr0_c0_s_cb_nomerge19`, `20`, `30` and `31_meshset_0` run
  from 15,462 to 29,738 units at their nearest to 190,929 to 520,733 at
  their farthest, and the ground `node000_world_lr0_agg19_3_hr_dust_ground_sand_skybox2`
  from 56,397 to 97,316. The skybox is imported at 16 times its size, as
  CS2 draws it from the `sky_camera`.
- `far.gdshaderinc` squeezes a far mesh's depth towards the far plane
  (`far_position`: `clip.z = far * w + (z - far * w) * 0.001`), which keeps
  what is inside the far plane behind the map. It cannot bring back what is
  beyond it: Godot's culling on the CPU drops a mesh whose box lies past
  the far plane before any shader runs, and a vertex beyond the far plane
  is still beyond it after the squeeze, so it is clipped.
- Raising `camera.far` is not the fix. At 100,000 the whole view went
  greyer and the hill nearly white: the fog's aerial perspective
  (`fog_aerial_perspective` 1.0, `map_lighting.gd`) samples the sky's
  radiance at a mip chosen by depth against `Camera3D.far` (Godot's
  Environment docs), so moving the far plane changes every fogged pixel.
- The fog is CS2's own and does not hide the hill: `env_cubemap_fog`'s
  maximum opacity 0.5 from 512 to 9,000 units (`map_lighting.gd`), so
  anything at any distance stays at least half seen, hazy as in CS2's
  shot. The skybox prefab has no fog of its own (its entities: the
  `sky_camera`, `env_sky`, `light_environment`, two probe volumes, a
  `func_brush` and `worldspawn`).

**Depends on.** Nothing. It touches `src/map/far.gdshaderinc`,
